=============Contract: LevErc20
Sink:
Reasoning:
- The contract has no constructor. `initialize` is public and uses `initializer`; the modifier only limits repeated initialization and does not restrict who calls it. It writes `admin = payable(msg.sender)` and critical state (`underlying`, `borrowUnderlying`, NAV-related variables). This is an unprotected initializer sink.
- `setDepErc20` is public and calls `super.setDepErc20(depErc20_)` without an admin/role check in this function. It writes the `depErc20` address, which is used as the authorization address for `forceRepay` and `updateLedger`. This is an unprotected privileged setter sink.
- `setPrologue` and `sweepToken` are protected by `require(msg.sender == admin)`, so they are not access-control sinks here.
- `forceRepay` and `updateLedger` are protected by `require(msg.sender == address(depErc20))` in this source context; the unprotected `depErc20` setter is the sink.
- `mint` and `redeem` are public user entry points that operate on the caller’s own deposited/redeemed assets; the listed state writes are protocol accounting, not access-control sinks.
- `doTransferIn` and `doTransferOut` are internal ERC-20 transfer wrappers; `transferFrom` relies on allowance and `transfer` sends to the caller/protocol-controlled recipient in the shown context. The assembly usage alone is not an access-control sink.
- View/getter functions do not write privileged state.
